Adzuna(https://publicapis.io/adzuna-api) offers instant, self-serve developer signup — you register at developer.adzuna.com and get a free App ID and key right away, and its coverage includes South Africa as one of its indexed countries, with each country accessible as a separate index you select via a country field, returning structured job data — title, company, location, salary, category, and apply link. Adzuna aggregates from thousands of underlying sources, so a decent chunk of SA listings (including ones that also appear on sites like Pnet or Careers24) will already be in there without you needing a deal with each individual board.

------------------------SHORT TERM ----------------------
For PERN, here's the natural shape:

Scheduling: Skip external cron services — use node-cron inside your Express app, or if you want it decoupled from your API server, a separate small worker process running on the same box/container. Either works fine at MVP scale.

Push: Use the web-push npm library on the Node side. You'll generate VAPID keys, store each user's push subscription object in Postgres, and loop through matching users when new jobs come in. Standard Service Worker + Push API on the frontend, nothing exotic.

Postgres schema, roughly:

users (id, email/auth, created_at)
job_preferences (user_id, title, location, created_at) — one user could have multiple
push_subscriptions (user_id, endpoint, keys jsonb)
job_matches (id, user_id, adzuna_job_id, title, company, url, notified_at) — dedup table so you don't re-notify on the same listing

The match job itself: for each row in job_preferences, hit Adzuna's /search endpoint, diff results against job_matches on adzuna_job_id, insert new ones, fire push for each. Batch/throttle this given the free tier's call limit — group preferences by title+location combo rather than looping per-user if multiple users want the same thing, so you're not burning calls on duplicate queries.

One thing to design for early: since Adzuna's free tier is small, you'll want a last_checked_at per preference so you're not hammering the same query more than once a day, and probably a way to prioritize which preferences get checked first once you have more users than your quota allows.

--------------------------LONG TERM------------------------


1. Getting officially zero-rated (the real "no data cost at all" route)

This isn't something you can just flip on — it's regulated by ICASA. As part of the 2022 spectrum auction conditions, mobile operators (Vodacom, MTN, Telkom, Rain, Cell C, Liquid) are legally required to zero-rate content from approved Public Benefit Organisations — non-profits, trusts, and government departments — with a compliance deadline of January 15, 2027.

To qualify:

You (or your app) need PBO status. That likely means registering as an NPO in South Africa, not just building an app as an individual — a for-profit or unregistered hobby project won't qualify even if it's free to use.
Content must be hosted inside South Africa on a South African domain — hosting on a US-based Vercel/AWS region wouldn't qualify without local hosting.
There's a streamlined route via DGMT's Social Innovation Register (SIR), which vets an organisation's PBO/tax status and technical eligibility for zero-rating — it's processed over 120 applications so far and is explicitly designed to make this process easier than going straight through ICASA.
Individual operators also run their own parallel programs — e.g. Vodacom's ConnectU, where you submit your URL directly for review — worth applying to alongside the ICASA/DGMT route since coverage across networks isn't automatic even once approved for one.
Expect restrictions: fair-usage caps apply even once zero-rated (MTN's PBO zero-rating, for example, caps usage around 500MB/month before standard rates kick back in), and rich media is capped at 480p.

Practical implication for you right now: this is a multi-month process that requires you to first exist as a registered non-profit entity. It's worth starting the paperwork early since you clearly want the free/community-good structure anyway, but don't block your MVP launch on it — build for low data use regardless, and treat zero-rating as a milestone you pursue once you have traction to point to.

2. Being cheap on data by design (do this immediately, no registration needed)

Independent of official zero-rating, you can make the app genuinely light:

No images/logos in the core flow — job listings as plain text, skip company logos, icons as inline SVG (tiny) not image files
Minimize JSON payload size — return only the fields you need from Adzuna, not full descriptions, in your own API responses to the frontend
Cache aggressively via service worker — PWA already gives you this; once installed, static assets shouldn't re-download on every visit
Consider WhatsApp as your notification channel instead of, or alongside, push — WhatsApp itself is sometimes bundled free or cheap on SA prepaid data plans, which is part of why ejoobi uses it. A WhatsApp Business API integration for "new job match" alerts could reach users more reliably and cheaply than requiring them to open a PWA on a live connection.
Avoid heavy JS frameworks bloat — code-split aggressively, lazy-load anything not needed on first paint

Between these two: pursuing PBO/zero-rating status is your long-term structural advantage (matches what SA Youth has), but the lightweight-by-design work is what actually protects your users' data costs from day one, regardless of whether the zero-rating paperwork comes through.
